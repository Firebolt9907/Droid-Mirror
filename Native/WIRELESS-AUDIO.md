# Opt-in phone audio on the automatic wireless mirror

On Android 12+ the companion samples music activity and media routing during an authenticated wireless video session. If the phone’s active network reports Wi-Fi, media is active, no Bluetooth media output is selected, and the phone is not in a call, a floating **Play phone audio over Wi-Fi** button appears below the mirror. Starting, cancellation, playback and retry have separate button labels. The panel follows moves/resizes, preserves keyboard focus and disappears during connecting screens, rotation presentation, inactive windows and ended sessions. Where the phone reaches the bottom of the display, the button stays inside the visible screen.

Android 13+ uses `AudioManager.getAudioDevicesForAttributes(USAGE_MEDIA)` rather than treating every connected output as the active route. Android 12 conservatively suppresses forwarding whenever a Bluetooth output is connected. During forwarding the remote submix changes routing, so a connected Bluetooth output also stops capture conservatively. Music activity and mode come from `AudioManager`; transient effects and apps not reported as music may not trigger the button. See the [Android AudioManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/AudioManager).

## Transport and lifecycle

- PCM uses a separate audio socket, independent of the video ACK window and decoder backpressure. Audio prefers a distinct paired, encrypted QUIC connection (`droidmirror/audio/1`) even when video uses wireless ADB. If that audio handshake fails, an ADB video session can fall back to its separate local audio forwarding channel. ADB forwards still share the underlying ADB connection, so they remain susceptible to video congestion. Both use the existing paired identity and an additional fresh, direction-separated `DMAUDIO1` proof handshake. Capture starts only after mutual proof and explicit receiver acceptance. Cellular-only active networks suppress the audio offer even when video can continue over a VPN. It is never carried by Classic, BLE, the video decoder, or the video replay/acknowledgement byte sequence. The underlying paired transport and handoff proofs remain unchanged.
- After video receiver confirmation (`1`), the Mac opts into audio state records with command `4`. Only then does the phone send state records. Older Macs never opt in; older phones ignore the extra command and keep video working. Legacy commands `2`/`3` still support old multiplexed clients. New clients start capture through the independent audio handshake and Stop by closing that lane. Eligibility state remains on video; PCM has its own bounded parser and no dependency on video progress.
- Envelope `6` contains one state byte: eligible `1`, sending `2`, capture failure `4`. Envelope `7` contains an unsigned big-endian capture PTS in microseconds followed by 48 kHz stereo signed 16-bit little-endian PCM (1–1024 frames). The complete payload is at most 4104 bytes. Only the Wi-Fi parser installs audio callbacks; Classic/BLE reject these records.
- The companion rechecks eligibility before starting. Its existing Shizuku owner token must still own a live video session before opening output capture. It reuses scrcpy's output source; it never selects a microphone. Android 11's foreground-activity capture workaround is deliberately excluded from this locked-phone path.
- Shell capture, pipe reader, playback engine and UI are scoped to the mirror session. Pause, Bluetooth output, call mode, user Stop, capture EOF, Wi-Fi fallback and session teardown release audio. The two-minute background inactivity timeout dismisses the mirror while requested audio continues, with a speaker menu bar icon labeled “Phone audio → This Mac”. Its menu offers Show Phone and Stop Casting. Showing the phone (including reopening the app) removes the icon and resumes the visible mirror; Stop Casting or audio ending while hidden closes the inactive session. The authenticated video/control session remains connected behind the hidden surface because it owns capture; this does not reduce video transport traffic. Without requested audio, inactivity retains its ordinary full-session stop behavior. Audio is opt-in again after recovery; it does not silently restart on reconnection. Audio initialization failures offer Retry without ending video. Startup and missing-packet deadlines bound failure waits; Stop retries a congested command for at most one second before recovering video to Bluetooth.
- The Shizuku daemon code version is bumped to 49 so new audio Binder methods cannot accidentally use an older daemon. End active mirrors before installing either component; use the existing graceful quit/update workflow.

## Timing and limits

PCM avoids an additional encode/decode delay at about 1.54 Mbps, suitable for the wireless path. An `AVAudioSourceNode` pulls continuously from a single-producer/single-consumer ring rather than waiting for output-device playback completions. A minimum 240 ms jitter reserve absorbs observed delivery gaps of up to about 180 ms. A real starvation increases the reserve by 60 ms, capped at 360 ms, rather than repeatedly restarting with an insufficient reserve. This adds audio delay in exchange for continuity; it is not a video synchronization guarantee. Small sample-rate corrections account for clock drift. A large backlog is trimmed with a short fade without stopping/restarting the audio engine. Starvation re-buffers once; queue/underrun/trim diagnostics and maximum arrival/capture timestamp gaps are emitted every five seconds. The render callback does not allocate, lock, dispatch or call blocking APIs. [Apple's source-node documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosourcenode/init(format:renderblock:)) describes the real-time constraint; [played-back completion callbacks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioplayernodecompletioncallbacktype/dataplayedback) also include output hardware latency and are unsuitable as the queue occupancy counter.

Capture latency and Mac output hardware still affect lip sync. This is low-latency forwarding, not a shared hardware A/V presentation clock or guaranteed synchronization. Protected apps and OEM capture restrictions may produce silence or reject capture. Actual routing, audible sound quality and lip sync require a live phone test.

## Phone speaker and volume lifecycle

The selected scrcpy `OUTPUT`/`REMOTE_SUBMIX` source exclusively redirects playback, silencing the phone speakers without setting the captured media stream's volume to zero. It does not use the duplicating playback source. The shell saves the media stream's last audible volume and mute flag before capture, including the nonzero slider value of an already muted phone. After capture releases its route, it restores that state on Stop, failure, route loss, session release, app death and guard recovery. The guard retries temporary restoration failures and rejects a new audio capture until the old release completes. A new headset route keeps its own volume; the previous device's remembered volume is left intact. Ringer, alarms, calls and DND are not changed. Android releases the remote route automatically if the shell process itself dies; no persistent zero-volume setting is introduced.

The button belongs to **Automatic (Bluetooth → Wi-Fi)** mode. The legacy USB / wireless ADB mode retains its existing scrcpy audio preference.

## Verification

`bash Native/test-wireless-audio.sh` checks fragmented/invalid wire data, PCM stereo conversion, three minutes of simulated burst delivery, jitter reserve, clock correction, underflow/overflow recovery, stop, floating button placement/state/focus, timeout casting indicator, resume/stop actions, route loss and session teardown. It uses a fake player and in-memory menu bar registration, not a claim of audible output or a live menu bar integration test.

`bash Native/test-wireless-upgrade.sh` includes the production endpoint over JVM socket substitutes: legacy opt-out, authenticated opt-in, explicit Start/Stop, no PCM in Classic/replay, Wi-Fi loss, existing proof/sequence/recovery tests and native helper compilation. These need permission to open local sockets; UI tests need the macOS window server.

Compile the companion with `ANDROID_HOME="$HOME/Library/Android/sdk" JAVA_HOME="$(/usr/libexec/java_home -v 21)" ./gradlew --offline --no-daemon compileDebugJavaWithJavac -x buildPeerTransport` in `BluetoothCompanion`. Build both app components before device testing. No install or running-session replacement is performed by the test scripts.

`bash Native/test-mac-update.sh` checks graceful Mac app/helper shutdown, refusal handling, installed-bundle resume targets, atomic publish/rollback and Android media-volume restoration (muted, zero, failed start, route release, new headset and retry).

The independent-lane regression tests exercise the actual native Mac handshake against the production Java endpoint, including fresh pairing proofs, receiver acceptance before capture, PCM framing, idle Android socket semantics, EOF cleanup and Wi-Fi-only admission. The handshake has a bounded read timeout; after acceptance, the reverse direction intentionally stays idle until EOF. Android LocalSocket reports a read timeout as plain IOException/EAGAIN, so streaming must disable that timeout. Closing the candidate releases the blocking read during stop or video fallback. Rust tests hold video open without progress and verify audio still crosses a separate paired QUIC connection.
